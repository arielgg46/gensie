\section{Experimental Setup}

The submitted solution consists of three final pipelines: \texttt{Schema-RAG}, \texttt{Reasoned-RAG}, and \texttt{Mixed-SC}. These correspond to the submitted names \path{enriched-schema-rag}, \path{enriched-inline-reasoning-rag}, and \path{mixed-extractors-self-consistency-rag}. All three use the retrieval and schema-rendering mechanisms described above.

We report the organizer-provided official GenSIE results. The official scoring set contains 125 instances: the released 145-instance gold test set minus a uniform 20-instance drop-set. The excluded groups are 8 \texttt{medical\_trials}, 2 \texttt{cultural\_monuments}, and 10 \texttt{stem\_biology} instances, removed because their schemas could not be scored reliably in the constrained-decoding inference stack. The exclusion is applied uniformly to all systems and to both baselines \cite{gensie2026overview}.

The official leaderboard uses micro-averaged precision, recall, and \(F_1\) over flattened schema leaves. It ranks systems by the average model-specific gap closed,
\[
  \max\left(0, \frac{F_1^{system} - F_1^{baseline}}{1 - F_1^{baseline}}\right),
\]
using fixed no-think baselines: \(F_1=0.7895\) for Gemma 4 E4B and \(F_1=0.7805\) for Qwen3-14B. For each model, the leaderboard takes the best reported cell for a system, regardless of whether that cell was run in \texttt{think} or \texttt{no-think} mode, but always computes gap closed against these no-think baselines.

We do not report wall-clock-time comparisons. The official reports do not isolate our code from hosted backend latency and small-language-model generation time. Token counts are also omitted from the main tables because the ranking is quality-based and the official summary metrics are computed after the 20-instance filter.

After the drop-set, the scored instances contain 20 unique structural schema fingerprints under the same normalization used by retrieval. Nine fingerprints are present in the development/FSP corpus and account for 34 scored instances; 11 are unseen and account for 91 scored instances. The retrieval replay routes the 34 seen-fingerprint instances to same-schema retrieval and the remaining 91 to field-aware fallback. No retrieval-failure route remains after the official exclusions.

\subsection{Reasoning and Self-Consistency}

Following the best-cell comparison in Table~\ref{tab:best-pipeline-cells}, the temporary reasoning/value interface is an inference-time variant of the shared RAG extractor, but the official cells do not show it overtaking direct \texttt{Schema-RAG}. Best-cell \texttt{Reasoned-RAG} reaches \(F_1=0.8181\) with Gemma and \(F_1=0.8297\) with Qwen, below \texttt{Schema-RAG} in both models. The no-think \texttt{Reasoned-RAG} cells are also below the corresponding \texttt{Schema-RAG} no-think cells for the models where both are available.

\texttt{Mixed-SC} is the best official Gemma cell because the \texttt{think} \texttt{Mixed-SC} run edges ahead of Gemma \texttt{Schema-RAG} by \(+0.0029\) \(F_1\). This does not support a broad claim that self-consistency is robustly beneficial. With Qwen, best-cell \texttt{Mixed-SC} is lower than best-cell \texttt{Schema-RAG} by \(-0.0169\) \(F_1\). In like-for-like no-think comparisons, \texttt{Mixed-SC} is below \texttt{Schema-RAG} for both models: .8165 versus .8211 with Gemma, and .8129 versus .8346 with Qwen. The extra compute and blind resampling in \texttt{Mixed-SC} therefore appear useful for one Gemma thinking cell, but not as a consistent default.

\subsection{Failure Patterns}

The main official scoring caveat is the 20-instance drop-set \cite{gensie2026overview}. It removes two seen-schema \texttt{MonumentBIC} instances and 18 unseen-schema instances from \texttt{ClinicalTrialRecord} and \texttt{TaxonomicClassification}. After this filter, the retrieval replay has no failed retrieval routes, and all 91 fallback instances have field-level pair diagnostics. The remaining errors are semantic and structural extraction errors inside scored instances rather than unscoreable schema-stack failures.

We keep per-domain tables, selected-example hub counts, and retrieval-score distributions in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-retrieval-diagnostics}. These diagnostics are useful for interpreting the submitted system, but they are not controlled causal ablations.
